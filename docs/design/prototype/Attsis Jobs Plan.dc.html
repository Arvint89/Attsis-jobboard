Product plan · September 23, 2026 · Stage 0

Attsis Jobs — from job board to job-search tool

Nine capabilities, in the order they should ship, with the architecture decision they force and how each one makes money. The clickable prototype is Attsis Job Board v3: Roles, Tracker, Tailor, Prep, Learn and Profile.

The one rule

Ranking is never for sale. Ads, affiliate links and sponsors live in Learn, Prep and the digest — never in the role list, the apply flow, tailoring or the tracker.

2. The decision this forces

Steps 1–3 run on today's architecture: a static site, a free daily GitHub Action, and scoring in the browser. The rest does not.

  • Steps 5, 7, 8 need accounts and a small backend. Store keywords, preferences, tracker rows and channel IDs — never the CV file. "Nothing uploads" becomes "your CV file never leaves your device".
  • Steps 4 and 6 need an LLM. Keep them paid-only so token cost tracks revenue. Accept ADR-002 (the model router) before building either.

Decide both before Phase 3. Everything before then works without them.

Teardown content — four options

  1. Employer-product teardowns as interview prep. "Know their product" in the Prep tab. Most relevant, and it ties content to the board.
  2. In-house teardowns (Attsis Media). One a month with P.Eng credibility, each cut into 3–4 shorts for TikTok and YouTube.
  3. Community teardowns. The creator program. Cheapest to scale; needs a review step before anything goes live.
  4. Sponsored product teardowns. A brand sends a new product; the teardown is labelled "Sponsored" and the brand gets no edit rights over findings.

Start with 1 and 2 in Phase 5, open 3 in Phase 6, and sell 4 once the digest reaches about 5,000 readers.

5. Compliance to plan for early

  • CASL — express opt-in per channel, sender identified, one-tap unsubscribe, and a record of when and how consent was given.
  • PIPEDA — store keywords and tracker data only, delete on request, say where it is hosted.
  • Disclosure — label affiliate links and sponsored posts, including creators' own posts (Competition Bureau guidance on material connections).
  • AI — say where AI is used in tailoring and prep; never add skills the CV doesn't have.
  • WhatsApp — business verification and pre-approved message templates before launch.

6. What to measure

  • Strong fits per user per week
  • Applications logged, and "heard back?" prompts answered
  • Interview rate: tailored vs master CV
  • Free-to-paid conversion, and revenue by stream
Part 2 · Employer side, sources and scale

7. Employer side — opt-in matching, never selling profiles

Seekers pay for a few months and leave when hired. Employers pay every time they hire. The employer side is where durable revenue sits — but only with consent. Selling profiles would breach PIPEDA and the product's promise.

  1. Seeker opts in to "Open to intros", per role type. Off by default.
  2. Employer posts a role for free. It feeds the board, and a salary range is required.
  3. Employer sees ranked, anonymous matches: fit score, matched skills, gaps, salary expectation, availability, clearance level. No name, no CV.
  4. Employer sends an intro request. The seeker accepts or declines. Identity and CV are shared only on accept.
Employer offer What they get Proposed price
Post a roleListed on the board, map and alerts; structured job-posting markup so Google lists it tooFree
MatchesRanked opted-in candidates for each active role, with gap view$53.99/mo per role
Accepted introPay only when a candidate says yes$89.99 each
Hire feeFlat fee instead of an agency's percentage of salary$1,799.99
  • Liquidity first. Employers won't pay for an empty pool. Open a hub only once about 1,000 active seekers there have opted in.
  • Guardrails. Consent is per employer and can be withdrawn. Employer money never changes the seeker's ranking. Seekers are never charged for placement.
  • Legal check before launch. Ontario now licenses recruiters. Confirm with a lawyer whether a paid matching service counts.

9. Security-clearance roles

Yes, it's needed. The registry already holds defence employers in the region (General Dynamics Land Systems in London, Safran, Amphenol, WOLF, Ultra Maritime). Clearance is a hard gate: showing a Secret role to someone who can't get cleared wastes an application. For cleared engineers it's the opposite — they're scarce, and highlighting these roles is a reason to use the board.

Posting says Your profile Board shows
Active Secret requiredSecret or Top Secret"Clearance match" chip, ranked up
Ability to obtain Reliability or SecretNone yet, eligible"Employer sponsors clearance" — shown normally
Active clearance requiredNone"Needs active Secret" — moved down, dimmed
Controlled Goods, ITAR, or citizens onlyNot eligibleHidden by default; "Show roles I may not qualify for" brings them back
Any clearance termNot answeredNeutral chip naming the level, e.g. "Secret clearance"
  • Detection is rule-based and runs in the same pass as salary and arrangement: Reliability Status, Secret, Top Secret, Controlled Goods Program, ITAR/EAR, "citizens only", and "active" vs "ability to obtain".
  • Profile field is optional: clearance held, Controlled Goods registration, eligibility. It's self-declared, stays in the browser, and is never guessed from a name or CV.
  • Wording: repeat only what the posting requires. Never imply the board decides who is eligible.

10. Where AI fits — and why this isn't just a wrapper

Today there is no AI in the product: fetching, scoring and gap checks are plain code. AI should be added only where rules can't do the job, and the expensive parts should sit behind the paywall.

Task Method Cost Tier
Fetch, dedupe, score, short gap, clearanceDeterministic Python + browser JS$0Free
Synonyms ("firmware" ≈ "embedded software")Small embedding model, once per posting, cachedFractions of a centFree
Pulling salary, clearance and arrangement from messy postingsRules first; small model only when rules miss; cached per postingFractions of a centFree
Tailored CV and cover letterLarge model, plus a check that no new facts were addedCents per rolePaid
Mock interviews, feedback, negotiationLarge model chat, grounded in the posting, gaps and salary dataCents per sessionPaid

A prompt over someone else's model can be copied in a weekend. The moat is everything around it:

  • source coverage and the company registry
  • explainable scoring
  • tracker outcome data — what actually gets interviews
  • creator content
  • employer liquidity

The ADR-002 router keeps model choice swappable.

This is small data. One managed Postgres database handles all of Canada for years. The first thing to break isn't storage. It's the single jobs.json file the browser downloads. Past roughly 2,000–5,000 roles it gets too heavy, so the board needs a search API that returns only the roles in view.
